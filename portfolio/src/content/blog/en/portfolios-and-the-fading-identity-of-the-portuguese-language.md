Studying the languages of other places and other times reveals a curious phenomenon in them, born of the meeting of cultures (and tongues): the trading of words.\
\
You can see this phenomenon at work quite easily in any globalised setting. In the corporate world, symbols show up as layers of overlapping meaning, and the foreign one is always the favourite. Nobody in Brazil holds a _reunião_ any more, only a meeting; and voice calls have given way to _calls_.\
\
Still in a corporate context, a phenomenon derived from this borrowing also shows up in the English word "portfolio". Now, this word has no direct translation into Portuguese in the sense given to it here. Portfolio, in the context at hand, means a collection, in any format, of records and presentations of someone's work, study and any other relevant activity, with a view to sharing their capabilities with a client or an employer.\
\
In Brazil, this term was Portuguesed — lazily — as "portfólio". That is, an acute accent was added. So now I take the liberty (the essay being mine) of writing in a more personal tone to ask you, reader: how do we accent a word?\
\
Well, a decent first step is to break it into syllables. Let's do that with "portfólio":

> POR · T · FÓ · LIO

> POR · T · FÓ · LI · O

(Note that the constructions PORT · FÓ · [...] were not preferred here because of the imperfect consonant cluster.)\
\
It can be either a paroxytone with a rising diphthong or a proparoxytone ending in a hiatus. It really should carry an accent. But there is something odd about that syllable break, and I imagine it is a fairly noticeable detail: we have a lone letter "T" in the middle of the word.\
\
Let's take a brief look at another word with a mute T: _deficit_. Another loanword, it lost the accent it used to have ("déficit") when it was Portuguesed, precisely because there was no syllable break (or morphological structure) to justify it. Look:

> DE · FI · CI · T

There is an incompatibility with the Portuguese language in this word. Given that its stressed syllable is the fourth from the end (DE), it follows that it is neither an oxytone nor a paroxytone, of course, but not a proparoxytone either.\
\
There is, in Portuguese, no such thing as stress on a syllable before the antepenultimate one. _Deficit_ is therefore a loanword that never received a corresponding term in the language, as happened, for example, with _leiaute_ (layout) and _esqueite_ (skate).\
\
"Portfolio", by contrast, has a structure entirely compatible with Portuguese morphology. As mentioned, it is a paroxytone (the preferred reading in Brazil, though more readily taken as a proparoxytone in Portugal) and the phoneme of the third syllable is easily adapted by adding the letter I, as follows:

> POR · TI · FÓ · LIO

Beautiful. Perfect. A fine construction in which you can barely tell it derives from a loanword. Entirely in keeping with Portuguese morphology.\
\
This text is also a plea to the Brazilian Academy of Letters to recognise "portifólio" as the official spelling (or its Lusitanian variant, _portefólio_) of the term in the Orthographic Vocabulary of the Portuguese Language.
