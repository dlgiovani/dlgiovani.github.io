Who has never bought something outrageously expensive online, only to find out right afterwards that the shop was about to run the sale of the century? Well, just like physical shops, online businesses are always moving their prices around.\
\
Not only that: it is very common to find an item in one online shop at one price and then, when we visit another site, what looks like the same product carries a **completely different price**. So what now? Is it a scam? Is the other shop charging extortionate amounts? Below, let's talk a little about **how to tell extortionate prices from good prices, and good prices from scams**.

## 1. Buy from Trustworthy Sources

Give preference to shops you already know or that were recommended by people you trust. Although large shops such as Amazon, Pichau and AliExpress are well known and established, **it is equally important to support smaller, local traders with a good reputation**. Established shops, regardless of size, tend to deal with customers more **transparently** online, which can guarantee a better **purchase and after-purchase experience** for the user.\
\
Before buying from an unknown shop, look up reviews from other consumers on specialised sites and check the company's reputation. Spreading your purchases across different trustworthy suppliers helps promote a healthier and more competitive market.

## 2. Don't Trust Links

Let's suppose you saw a link to a product on Amazon in an internet forum. The link was something like this:

```
amаzon.com.br/someProductGoingCheap
```

**It looks perfectly legitimate**, so you click the link and an Amazon page opens. There is an 80-inch TV on it for R$ 89.90, you get excited and count your blessings. You pay by instant transfer and wait for the delivery.\
\
There is just **one problem**. Three days go by and **still no news of your TV**. You now type amazon.com into the browser, log into your account and find **no record whatsoever of a TV purchase**. You panic and try to find the sale listing. Nothing. So you click the link you saw in the forum again, but **it doesn't work**. Not even if you keep only the domain (amаzon.com). You discover that **you fell for a scam**. But how is that possible?\
\
The link you clicked **was not Amazon's**, even though it looked like it. In fact, the second "a" in that link **is not from our alphabet**, but from the [Cyrillic alphabet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyrillic_script). It isn't on your keyboard and it is indistinguishable from a Latin "a", but **your computer tells them apart**. That link took you to a **cloned site**, almost perfectly identical to Amazon's, and it was **impossible to notice the difference**.\
\
To avoid falling for these scams, **always type the site's address yourself**. It is the **best way** to protect yourself from this.

## 3. Join Deal Groups and Forums

This tip is **worth its weight in gold**. Deal groups and forums are excellent resources for anyone who wants to keep up with **coupons and sales** on the internet. Such groups tend to target products in one specific niche or in several, and cover deals on **products**, offers on **travel and accommodation** and much more. There are plenty of trustworthy groups on platforms such as Telegram, Discord and specialised forums that share verified offers daily.

## 4. Weigh Up Whether You Need Fast Shipping

While fast shipping **can be convenient**, it often comes with a significant extra cost. Consider whether the urgency of the delivery **justifies the extra cost** or whether you can pick a slower shipping option and save money. In many cases, a difference of a few days in delivery can add up to substantial savings.

## 5. Keep an Eye on Your Shopping Basket

Sometimes, leaving items in the basket for a few days can **lead to extra discounts**. Some online retailers **send emails** with discount coupons or offer **special deals** to encourage customers to complete their purchases. So, if you are not in a hurry, try **leaving items in the basket for a while** and see whether you get some **extra incentive**.\
\
Be careful, though: in some cases, especially during periods of high demand or limited-time sales, waiting too long can leave you with sold-out products or prices that climb back up.
